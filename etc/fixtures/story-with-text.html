CreepGin 1 day ago | root | parent | next [–]

Thanks! Haiku is doing the following:

1) tldr summary of each post (when a link is first submitted). 2) Deduping posts. 3) Detecting when there are multiple stories (different links) that are basically covering the same thing, in which case it'll "merge" them by using only the best post (it decides). 4) Come up with titles for posts that don't have them (like twitter/bluesky posts).

Jev is used for:

1) Quickly getting rid of bad posts during the 1st stage of crawling. 2) Determining initial amount of points (based on a scale) awarded to post when it was first submitted.

There are probably few other cases I'm not thinking of right now. But basically, the gist is to use Jev for super frequent classifier tasks that are early in the pipeline so it can save cost.

This will roughly use about $40 of my AI credits per month. Anthropic gives Ultra users $200 monthly credit for API usage. So I'm happy I can make some good use out of it.

thisoneisreal 3 days ago | parent | prev | next [–]

I have the same experience, and I don't think it's just us.

I suspect what's going on is we're experiencing the long-term effects of AI recommendation algorithms getting plugged into everything. I'm not anti-AI. I think neural nets are incredibly powerful tools and I really like using them as an aide for my coding work. But they clearly do only a first or second level of pattern matching(asterisk), and I think that has a fundamental impact on their overall usefulness and desirability.

I can't explain this any better than Melanie Mitchell in her "AI for Thinking Humans," so I'd generally recommend that book to really understand the issues. But one way of thinking about it is that the AI keys in on patterns that are indeed there in its training data, but very often don't pick up on the same patterns as a human or treat patterns "not in the same way" as a person would. So you get the uncanny valley effect where the recommendations share some features of things you like and find interesting, but often miss the "soul" of the thing.

A good example of this is I recently watched the movie "X-Men" (2000). I then got a bunch of superhero movie recommendations. On the surface, this makes total sense. I watched a superhero movie, I get more superhero movies. But the reason I like that movie is it portrays racism/nationalism in a way that isn't totally stereotyped while still being PG-rated and easy to watch, without the seriousness of something like "American History X" (it was also a Patrick Stewart tribute). It's very unlikely that an AI can make that level of inference. I actually would enjoy watching more movies like that (as opposed to your point about wanting variety and something more exploratory), but what are the odds it's going to recommend some vague theme like that instead of "more superheroes"?

I also agree with your point that the underlying assumption we all want "more of the same" is a big thing that makes the modern internet feel more narrow. So you put those 2 together and it feels like we're being put into a narrow Skinner box that approximates aspects of things we like, but is really more of a confined environment designed to extract our time and attention while only fulfilling the shallowest, barest minimum of our wants and desires.

dang 3 days ago | parent | next [–]

HN's Firebase-based API (https://github.com/hackernews/api) makes that easy, until you want to reconstruct the threads (i.e. who replied to who) and then it's annoying.

For years* I've wanted to replace that API with one we operate ourselves, which will simply return a JSON version of any HN URL.

I've finally started working on this! Perhaps my favorite thing about LLMs is how they make projects possible which would otherwise not have gotten started.

Edit: perhaps I should explain what I mean by that last bit.

The way I want this new API to work is (1) HN should use it internally to generate HTML pages, and (2) there can't be any regressions - nothing should change in the web version. This is a pain to get right, as it's easy subtly alter the details, and it's annoying to regression-test everything. Also, (3) with a design like this, it is easy—and critical not to—leak data that ought to remain private (e.g. people's IP addresses)

This is the sort of pain and annoyance that LLMs are amazing at taking care of. I get to focus on the parts I want to think about—the design and (yes) the code—and have a tireless assistant do the gruntwork and review. Without this I don't think I'd have gotten around to working on it.

* (edit: over a decade in fact - see collapsed reply)

Changelog push from HN comments on this thread:

- Filters on search and topic pages: upload date, duration, type (talk, lecture, documentary, ...), and sort by upload date, view count, etc. (ty jahala, kiuiras, mixtureoftakes)

- HackerTube Longs: every video over an hour, right where YouTube puts Shorts. (ty gosen)
